02 / TRANSLATE INTO PRACTICE

Exercise examples for clinical care

Explore practical activity ideas across aerobic conditioning, strength, balance, and gait. Each example can be adapted to the person, setting, and level of support needed.

Examples are clinician-designed illustrations of the intervention categories discussed in the review—not exact tested protocols from the included trials.

First, assess safety and individual needs.

Consider cardiovascular status, fatigue, pain, fall risk, communication ability, supervision, prescribed assistive devices, and the person's own goals. Stop and obtain clinical assessment for concerning symptoms.

01ILLUSTRATIVE ACTIVITY
AerobicAmbulatory

Walking with simple cues

Practice comfortable walking along a clear, familiar route.

How to guide & adapt +

Simple cue
Invite the person to walk with you; use short, one-step cues.

Adapt it
Shorten the route, take breaks, or use their prescribed walking aid.

Safety check
Watch for fatigue, wandering risk, obstacles, and changes in walking stability.

02ILLUSTRATIVE ACTIVITY
AerobicSeated

Seated marching

While sitting securely, alternate lifting one knee at a time.

How to guide & adapt +

Simple cue
Model the movement and use a steady, simple rhythm.

Adapt it
Use smaller lifts or alternate with seated arm movement.

Safety check
Ensure the chair is stable and feet can return safely to the floor.

03ILLUSTRATIVE ACTIVITY
StrengthSupported standing

Sit-to-stand

Stand from a sturdy chair and sit back with control, with assistance as needed.

How to guide & adapt +

Simple cue
Try “lean forward, stand tall, sit slowly”—one instruction at a time.

Adapt it
Use chair arms, a higher seat, or therapist assistance as indicated.

Safety check
Guard closely; confirm safe transfer ability and avoid unsteady unsupported attempts.

04ILLUSTRATIVE ACTIVITY
StrengthSeated

Seated band row

Pull a low-resistance band toward the torso while sitting upright.

How to guide & adapt +

Simple cue
Demonstrate “pull toward you” and then “relax.”

Adapt it
Begin without a band to learn the movement; choose resistance individually.

Safety check
Secure the band safely and avoid breath-holding or painful movement.

05ILLUSTRATIVE ACTIVITY
StrengthSupported standing

Supported heel raises

Hold a stable surface and slowly rise onto the toes, then lower.

How to guide & adapt +

Simple cue
Use a visual demonstration and a calm rhythm.

Adapt it
Use smaller lifts and clinician assistance, or adapt to seated ankle raises.

Safety check
Do not use an unstable chair or rolling surface for support.

06ILLUSTRATIVE ACTIVITY
BalanceSupported standing

Supported weight shifts

With hands near a stable support, gently move weight side to side.

How to guide & adapt +

Simple cue
Cue “this side” while pointing or demonstrating.

Adapt it
Start with small, comfortable movements and more hand support.

Safety check
Use close guarding and stop if balance control becomes unsafe.

07ILLUSTRATIVE ACTIVITY
BalanceSupported standing

Step to a target

Step toward a clearly marked target and return to the start position.

How to guide & adapt +

Simple cue
Offer a single visible target and one instruction at a time.

Adapt it
Place the target close; progress only after safe, consistent performance.

Safety check
Clear trip hazards; use guarding and support based on fall risk.

08ILLUSTRATIVE ACTIVITY
GaitAmbulatory

Walk and turn

Walk a short familiar path and practice a controlled turn at the end.

How to guide & adapt +

Simple cue
Use a simple cue such as “walk to the cone, then turn.”

Adapt it
Widen the turn, reduce distance, and add rest as needed.

Safety check
Assess turning stability and environmental hazards before attempting.

MAKING IT DEMENTIA-FRIENDLY

Simple changes
can improve engagement.

Use short, concrete, one-step instructions.
Model movements instead of relying only on verbal explanation.
Use a predictable environment, familiar routines, and rest breaks.
Prioritize dignity, enjoyment, choice, and a successful experience.
