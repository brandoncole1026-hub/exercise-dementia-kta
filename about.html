05 / ABOUT THIS PROJECT

Evidence made usable.

A knowledge-to-action educational website focused on exercise for people living with Alzheimer’s disease, other dementias, and mild cognitive impairment.

THE PROBLEM

Good evidence is only useful
when it reaches care.

The submitted project identifies dementia and mild cognitive impairment as common presentations at UPMC Shadyside, where changes in mobility, balance, strength, and independence can increase the challenges of daily living.

The project proposes sharing understandable evidence with clinical leadership and physical therapy staff, identifying barriers together, and evaluating whether appropriate exercise interventions become more consistently documented in care.

WHAT THIS SITE IS

A clinical learning resource,
not a substitute for assessment.

01

Evidence grounded

The numerical findings and implementation sequence come from the provided project document.

02

Honest about uncertainty

Cognitive outcomes are not guaranteed; exercise may still have substantial relevance for physical function.

03

Person-centered

Exercises should fit an individual's cognitive needs, movement ability, preferences, medical conditions, and safety.

Project status and attribution

This is a draft educational companion to the submitted “KTA Project Part 1: Exercise and Dementia” document. The document identifies UPMC Shadyside as the proposed clinical implementation setting. This public website is not an official UPMC publication and does not imply institutional approval, an established treatment policy, or completed evaluation.

Clinical exercise examples on this website are illustrative translations of the review’s exercise categories. They are not specific protocols copied from a particular included trial. No patient-identifying information, survey responses, or analytics are collected.
