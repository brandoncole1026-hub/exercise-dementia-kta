What does the research show?

A careful summary of the supplied systematic review: what improved, what varied, and what clinicians can reasonably take into practice.

THE REVIEW AT A GLANCE

12 trials. Four outcomes.
One important distinction.

Exercise was associated with more consistent gains in physical performance than in cognitive outcomes. The review included people with dementia, Alzheimer's disease, and mild cognitive impairment.

1,924participants
11 / 12studies with PEDro ≥ 6/10

How the project review was assembled: PubMed, MEDLINE, ScienceDirect, and Cochrane Library were searched September 19–December 5, 2025. The summary reports 168 records identified, 25 full texts assessed, and 12 randomized trials included, restricted to publications from the prior 10 years.

PHYSICAL PERFORMANCE

Reported measures included aerobic capacity, muscle strength or endurance, balance, and gait. This was the most consistently favorable outcome area.

WHAT THE REVIEW SUGGESTS

Multimodal.
Supervised.
Individualized.

The project recommends considering combinations of aerobic, resistance, balance, and gait training, rather than relying on one exercise type alone.

These are suggestions from the project review, not a universal exercise prescription. Clinical assessment and the individual's preferences take priority.

INTERPRETATION & LIMITATIONS

What we know—and what we don't.

Appropriate clinical takeaway

Exercise is a reasonable, person-centered intervention to support mobility and physical performance in dementia and MCI, with possible but uncertain cognitive benefits.

Why certainty is limited

Protocols, intensity, duration, participant characteristics, and measurement tools varied. The uploaded review restricted inclusion to studies published in the prior 10 years.

About the evidence: These figures and interpretations are transcribed from the project's review summary. They do not constitute a new independent systematic review or a recommendation for every patient. For additional context, consult the primary references and external clinical guidance.
